# Rules for groups, segments and subsegments

Read for generation or a boundary revision. Cite rule IDs in the debate. These are
decision rules, not a scoring system or a claim that segments are mutually exclusive.

## Product fit before audience generation

**F1 — Scope the offering.** Establish what the researched version can do and its limits
before exploring tasks and possible users. A free entry product, README or single feature
does not describe the full offering. Separate implemented/demonstrated capabilities,
owner claims and proposed capabilities; do not extend coverage while reasoning.

**F2 — Explain fit.** Before building profiles, explain a value opportunity using
[value reasoning](value-reasoning.md): a person's meaningful task and current approach,
success criterion and limitation, the specific contribution of product/data/capabilities,
comparison with a strong alternative, conditions of no advantage and an observable
difference. Then name the person/situation, nearest candidate and a decision consequence
of splitting or merging them. A produced artifact is not the whole value argument.
The current approach and extra value may be hypotheses, labeled at the assertion; neither
needs observed demand to be a coherent candidate. A list of professions with generic pain,
or the same value sentence repeated across roles, fails this account even if format-valid.
Several capabilities can serve a single practice; breadth of features creates no quota
of segments. “WHO before job” governs recognition/profile boundaries, not a prohibition
on capability-first exploration of a previously unknown audience.

**F3 — Use available applications.** Within permitted access, consult the workspace/index
and a small varied set of relevant current applications before relying on a short product
summary alone. Give both roles the same bounded packet with sources, contexts, outcomes
and limits. A saved implementation report is an application observation with its own
reported checks; it does not independently prove efficacy, buyers, payment or demand.
Missing access, an empty index and no relevant examples are distinct limitations. Do not
scan the full private corpus, copy private facts into reusable instructions, or demand
interviews before generating a logically supported hypothesis.

**F4 — Breadth is a parameter.** Default to a useful compact overview. If the owner
requests a breadth comparison, keep the product packet and acceptance criteria fixed:
compare direct overview generation with a broader pool followed by selection. The pool
size and overview size belong to that experiment (for example, roughly fifty to ten),
not a universal quota or authorization for extra calls. Keep the pool/dispositions in
review artifacts, explaining coverage and why candidates were retained/merged/deferred.
Review the selected current overview itself; critique of an old list or capability-only
scout cannot approve it. Selection for further study is never validation of demand.

## Group: a way to find related customer profiles

**G1 — Recognizable family.** Name the people, organizations or practices in familiar
language. State what unites its profiles and what sits outside. Derive groups from
the product's possible customers. No preset count, quota or universal industry taxonomy.

**G2 — Practice determines placement.** The person answering is not necessarily the
customer unit. A teacher can describe personal teaching, a center's process, production
of materials for colleagues or learning with their own children. Identify the practice
and beneficiary; record user, chooser, buyer and payer separately where known.
Offline, an office, a school subject, a tariff or a large pupil count cannot determine
placement alone. Unknown organizational practice means unknown placement.
Known practice can be placed as a potential customer even when use of our product
is unverified. Missing adoption/payment is an evidence gap, not a reason to put a
known organization outside an organizational family or an employee inside it.

**G3 — Navigation is not evidence.** A group is not a segment, an extra parent edge,
an evidence level or a market-size bucket. Do not organize the map by AI/expert/confirmed
status. Groups need not be legally exclusive: one person can have distinct practices.

## Segment: a recognizable practice with a useful boundary

**S1 — WHO before job.** Name a person or organization and describe what they actually
do or could coherently do. Recognition must be usable in recruitment: inclusion and
exclusion signs, including a plausible near neighbor. "People with heavy checking
load", "online users" and a feature name are insufficient definitions.

**S2 — Decision consequence.** Explain how the boundary changes at least one of:
the value offered, primary/additional jobs, success criteria, product choice,
buying process or implementation. Use a causal account: observed/proposed circumstance
→ changed requirement → possible product/commercial decision. State which links are
observed and which are hypotheses. Do not invent criteria thresholds or priorities.
Check product fit against named capabilities: a tool that creates exercises does not
therefore fully perform teaching a subject. A customer's broader job can remain the
purpose above the product's Core; capability coverage must not expand during synthesis.

**S3 — Compare neighbors.** Compare with the nearest profile before splitting. An age,
subject, location, company size, pain or purchased feature is context until a meaningful
difference is explained. A profession can be a useful profile when its practice and
value difference are described; there is no automatic demographic or job-based split.
Prefer a coherent broader profile over a tree of all parameter combinations.

**S4 — Overlap has a reason.** Separate practices can overlap at person level. Explain
the difference in work/beneficiary/buying context. Duplicate cards with the same
practice and decision consequences should be merged; mixed cases need explicit scopes.
Working for a school does not establish the school's use of our platform. A tutor
using our tool privately and another tool at work can have two contexts, only one of
which is observed usage of our product.

**S5 — Candidates remain visible.** Distinguish observed practice, team account,
reasoned potential audience and unknown. Do not use a minimum respondent count to
erase a coherent hypothesis. Incomplete identity, product attribution or organizational
scope can block membership/placement while preserving the candidate and its question.
An existing corpus is a map of the reviewed material, not an exhaustive market map.

## Subsegment: a strict narrowing with its own value difference

**C1 — Parent containment.** The child definition must imply the parent's inclusion
rules, including for hypothetical future members when there are no cases. Every
included case must also satisfy those rules. Test a counterexample. Authors of all courses cannot sit under language tutors;
language tutors launching additional programs for their own learners can. Similar job
names do not establish containment. If the parent is unknown, nesting is unresolved.

**C2 — Useful narrowing.** Name the additional observable circumstance and explain
how it changes jobs, criteria, choice or implementation. Mere shared tags or a subset
of survey answers do not justify a child. Several independent practices may buy ready
materials; buying alone need not create one universal segment or identical children.
No required depth. Each further level must pass C1 and C2 independently.

**C3 — Own profile and basis.** Parent and child each have their own profile, rationale,
jobs/criteria and sources. No inherited membership, demand, payment, evidence or priority.
Generic labels must not imply a wider scope than the cases or explicit hypothesis.

## Evidence and customer roles

**E1 — Attribute each assertion.** Facts, team statements, analytical inferences and
unknowns remain separate. A quote about a practice does not prove its proposed value
proposition. Hypothesis profiles can be accepted as logically coherent hypotheses;
"accepted after review" never means proven market demand.
Audit each consequential fact-like assertion against its own source passage, even if
the surrounding profile has a broad hypothesis/unknown disclaimer. Buying a lesson
observes a purchase; it does not by itself establish a platform-selection criterion.
Selling a course does not establish self-serve delivery, a live cohort or an unfamiliar
audience. Use the actual sentence, not a resemblance to a worked example.

**E2 — Separate roles and products.** User ≠ chooser ≠ payer. Choosing, searching,
owning an office, employer status or paying an assistant does not prove platform payment.
Competitor purchases do not establish our revenue; a tariff name does not prove use
of another product. A teacher-parent case does not establish a paying parent market.
Specify what is being chosen/paid for. Commissioning a lesson establishes a customer
for that labor, not the decision maker for the software. Using a platform (personally
or at work) does not establish who selected it.

**E3 — Count what was observed.** Materials, unique people and membership links are
different counts. Do not merge anonymous lookalikes without identity evidence. Sample
frequency does not establish market prevalence or criterion priority. Missing data is
unknown, not a negative answer. Preserve contradictory and insufficient cases.

**E4 — Reconcile and bound.** Give every old and new candidate an explicit disposition:
retain, rename, merge, split, defer or retire. Preserve source history. A critique must
identify a concrete rule/counterexample or missing fact, not a taste preference. When
the available evidence cannot decide, retain alternatives and a specific next check.

## Required decision record (report artifact, not extra graph schema)

For each group: stable ID/name, navigation rationale, included profile IDs, boundary.
For each segment/subsegment: ID/name, proposed group and parent (or unknown), WHO,
inclusion/exclusion, nearest neighbor, why separate, changed jobs/criteria/buying,
user/chooser/payer, assertion bases/source references, unresolved questions.
For each candidate: final disposition and its reason, critic review and lead resolution.
Use the existing customer-profile contract for persistence; keep review-only fields
in the report. One serious boundary/evidence error cannot be hidden by an average score.
